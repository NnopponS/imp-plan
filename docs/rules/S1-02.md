# Cooperative Puzzle · Season 1, Day 1 prize match

**Playable fan table.** Practice on your own or share one board with 2–16 human players. No bots, personal points or competing-team bracket.

## The relay

Fill a rectangular marquetry frame with every piece in the tray. The board grows with the room; each piece covers three to five edge-connected squares. Rotate by quarter turns. The host may enable mirroring before the match. The numbered tiles remain identifiable without color.

The active player has 20 seconds to place one tile or take one placed tile back into the tray. A placement must stay within the frame and cannot cover another piece. Either action hands control to the next seat immediately. If time expires, that seat passes. A disconnected seat cannot stop the clock.

Anyone can suggest a legal position during a turn. A suggestion neither reserves squares nor changes the score. A new suggestion replaces that seat’s previous one, and all suggestions clear at handoff. To adopt a suggestion, the active player selects it and presses **Place tile**.

The team earns one point per covered square. Filling the entire frame wins; otherwise the board closes after five minutes or after 20 plus three turns per seat, whichever comes first. The result records elapsed time, squares covered and turns consumed. There is no speed ranking within a team.

## Provenance and verification

[Netflix Korea’s official first-prize-match clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_YjqhsXF4bQ) establishes the televised teamwork context. Broadcast fidelity remains **partial**. The board construction, controls, proposal system, clocks, score and limits here belong to the fan game, not a reconstruction of the episode.

The host constructs a full tiling, checks that its cells cover the rectangle exactly once and discards the answer before sharing the shuffled tray. The independent solver and room tests exercise solvability, turns, timeouts and score. Browser room tests exercise the repository’s local test transport. A separate two-browser check using native PeerJS, WebRTC and local STUN confirmed that suggestions, placements and undo replicate on the same machine. Public signaling and cross-network connectivity were not tested.
