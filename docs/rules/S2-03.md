# S2-03: Unknown

Season 2, Day 2 Main Match (Episodes 3–4). **Playable fan reconstruction, not a broadcast-exact implementation.** Two to twelve occupied seats share one board; accountless practice adds one bot. Twelve players were present in the episode as identified by the recap; this does not make smaller fan tables broadcast variants.

The episode's exact map, rankings, timing, complete interaction priority, and elimination thresholds remain unverified here.

## How this table plays

- Every seat begins on a separate edge socket. Roll the cube one cell north, east, south or west. It always changes orientation as a physical cube. The six colors have symbols as well as colors. A single blocking cube may be pushed if the next cell is free; there is no chain pushing. A blocked seat passes. An expired timer also passes.
- The host secretly selects one trigger apiece for **Exile** (return to your own socket), **Echo** (one extra roll in the same turn), and **Shift** (turn the cube to a neighboring symbol). Trigger order is exile, shift, echo; a pushed cube only checks exile. If a push sends a rival to the center, that rival wins. Goal beats tile/cube effects; blocks and edge sockets cannot be ordinary destinations. The public record shows outcomes rather than the hidden selectors. A law is named after every two completed circuits; remaining secrets open at the end.
- First to the center wins. After ten circuits without a finish, Manhattan distance to the center decides the lead, shared when tied. No prizes, eliminations, or broadcast Pieces; table points are only distance-based scoring. Clock duration is set in the lobby. The bot selects from the same legal moves, visible board, revealed laws and observed events as a human player. It does not receive unrevealed selectors.
- Our original 7×7 mosaic, three replayable laws, timing, scoring, cap, solo opponent, lower seat counts, and deterministic orientation/shift procedure are fan choices. Preview and play use the same actual board and counters. The animated briefing demonstrates rolls and pushes using the actual fan engine, not a decorative animation.
