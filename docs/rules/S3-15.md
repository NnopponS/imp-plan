# S3-15: Tower of Equations

Season 3, Day 5 Prison Match. Playable **fan adaptation**: one in the untimed Workshop or 2–8 at the buzzer table, including optional party bots.

## Source and scope

[Netflix's Season 3 announcement](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/the-devils-plan-dead-again-teaser) identifies Dead Again. The host's explanation in the [S03E12 transcript mirror](https://subtitlely.com/subtitle/b50b8yjzyww4t0yiwuci7v9s) is the primary episode evidence as transcribed, cross-checked against an [alternate episode transcript](https://www.subtitlecat.com/subs/1813/The.Devil%27s.Plan.S03E12.en.html). These mirrors are not official rulebooks; the broadcast footage, complete block inventory, precise flip semantics, adjudication, and calculation edge cases were not independently verified. The episode evidence distinguishes this from Equation Pyramid and Equation Lights: it specifies a numeric value and floor count, moving or flipping the top bundle, public move-count declarations with an undercut window, and exact-count physical proof. First call receives or loses one point; the later lower claim receives or loses two. Stacks carry into the next round and seven points ends the match.

## Fan rules

The eight original blocks (plain 7 and 5; ×2, +2, +5, −4, ×3 and ÷3) are shuffled across four stands. Tap a stand, select 1–8 top blocks as available, and place that bundle on another stand. You can reverse the bundle or reverse a multi-block bundle in place as one move. The entire standing tower must be the exact height. A plain number must be on top; read the remaining signed blocks top-down with ×/÷ before +/−. The host evaluates reduced rational numbers, not floating-point approximations and not code.

The Workshop carries the stacks across three untimed targets; you may reset a round before settling it. Each round succeeds when a matching tower is built, or closes without a point if you exhaust 18 moves. Each target starts unsolved and has a verified route: enumerate legal transfers to depth two, or construct a matching stack by clearing one stand and lifting the required blocks onto it. The same host-approved state and art drive interactive play and the read-only Preview.

In a party: draft locally or declare 1–10 moves. The first call opens the challenge clock; each other seat may pass or declare once, strictly below the current call. When everyone has acted or the clock ends, the lowest caller builds publicly using the exact declared number of moves. Equal calls do not displace the first. A proof that reaches the target too early still has to spend its full budget. There is no second builder after a failed proof. Clock and move-budget failures cost the corresponding one or two points; if no one calls, the round scores zero. First to at least seven wins, otherwise highest after twelve rounds; equal scores share the lead. Quick clock offers 25/12/60 seconds to call/challenge/build; Standard offers 45/20/90. These shorter windows, cap, shared ties, inventory, optional bots, practice and browser-local planning are fan choices, not TV rules. A timed round does not freeze for a disconnected player.
