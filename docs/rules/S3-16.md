# S3-16 · Gradation

**Playable:** The Tide Cabinet, an original fan allocation duel. **Broadcast status:** partial. Original mode is not offered.

## At the cabinet

Two sides, Copper and Jade, each arrange the weights numbered 1 through 7 behind a screen. Tap a weight to place it in the outlined gate. Tap any gate to edit it; choosing an already-placed weight swaps the two positions. Use each weight exactly once and seal before the clock expires. An expired, unsealed tray is filled with a seeded permutation, not the local unfinished draft.

Once both sides seal, the host opens one gate at a time, from left to right. A heavier weight scores one point; equal weights score nothing and interrupt a streak. Three successive winning gates earn one extra point. A side can claim its streak bonus only once per round. Past trays stay in the public round ledger.

The optional **Marked gate** switch starts off. If enabled, each allocator must privately pick a gate before sealing. Both marks become public together when the first gate opens. Winning your own marked gate pays one extra point; a tie never pays. Example: Copper puts 6 against Jade’s 3 at Copper’s marked gate. Copper scores two, or three if it also completes a new three-gate streak.

Total points over five rounds decide the game. If the totals match, play exactly one more round with fresh trays. Equal totals after that are a draw.

## Seating & timing

- Practice needs no name or account. It puts you at Copper opposite one labeled bot. Easy picks a fresh permutation; Normal and Hard also compare candidate trays against the opponent’s last revealed arrangement. Bots never receive a current opponent lock or host game seed.
- A party of two plays head-to-head. For 3–16 players, alternating seats form two teams and a different allocator takes each round; everyone on the same side shares its final score. With more than ten participants, not all teammates will seal during five rounds. Larger teams start at a seeded offset.
- The host picks the allocation clock (45 seconds by default). Each open gate stays visible while the next opens; the completed round pauses before a fresh tray appears. A suspended host resumes the reveal from the last published gate. Reconnecting restores an acknowledged lock, not an unfinished local draft.
- Other seats see public gates only, never either unexposed tray. Each allocator can see its own sealed order. The host browser is authoritative, not a secure referee against a malicious host.
