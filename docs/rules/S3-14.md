# S3-14: Wall Go II

- Category: Prison D4
- Native seats: 2 or 3 on one shared board. Solo practice seats one bot by default; add a second bot for a triad.
- Broadcast verification: partial. Only the standalone fan game is available.

## Fan table

1. **Draft on a 7 × 7 board.** In a duel, four round pieces start at B2/F6 and F2/B6. Draft two square pieces per player in seat order 1, 2, 2, 1. In a triad, each player drafts one square and one round in either order. Seats act 1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1. Draft into any empty tile.
2. **Move, then build.** Squares take one or two orthogonal steps in the same direction. Rounds take one orthogonal step and may add one perpendicular step. Staying, jumping, and stopping on an occupied cell are not legal. A round can take either valid path around a corner. Build one wall on a previously open edge beside the destination. A turn is not committed until you choose that edge.
3. **Breach in a triad.** Each player can cross and remove one wall on their path once. They still place a fresh wall at the destination; the removed edge cannot be rebuilt in the same action. Duels have no breaches. If all your pieces are stuck, the table skips you.
4. **Watch the clock.** Each action has 90 seconds, including draft placements. When time runs out, the host picks a complete legal action, using a breach only if no normal action is available.
5. **Survey the fortress.** Play ends when no component contains pieces from different seats, even if a player has a breach left. It also ends when nobody can move and build. A connected room scores only when every piece there belongs to one seat and is counted once no matter how many of their pieces are inside. Vacant and mixed rooms are neutral. Highest total wins; if tied, compare the largest single room. An exact tie shares the win.

## Difference from S2-10

[S2-10 Wall Go](S2-10.md) is a separate fan duel with one pawn per player, no placement phase, staying allowed, permanent walls, and a 40-turn cap. Wall Go II uses multiple pieces with distinct routes, a snake draft, a triad with wall breaches, and largest-room tiebreaking. No round-robin wrapper or artificial turn cap is used: each move either adds a wall or consumes one of the three one-use breaches.
