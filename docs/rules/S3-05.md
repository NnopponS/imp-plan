# S3-05: Memory Poker

Category: Either Or mini-game
Playable: 1–16 human seats in Fan table (including solo); 2 connected humans in Episode-style duel
Broadcast verification: partial; neither mode represents a verified official rulebook.

## Fan table (default)

Study the same 4×5 layout of twenty different cards for 25 seconds. It is drawn from a standard 52-card deck. The cards turn face down automatically. In the next 30 seconds, choose five distinct positions and lock your hand. Other seats may choose the same positions without affecting your hand. Once locked, your hand cannot change.

When everyone has locked or time expires, reveal only cards in submitted hands for six seconds. A seat without a locked hand receives zero rank credit. Play four independent deals with fresh layouts. Standard five-card hand ranks and kickers determine the results. The ace can rank as low in A–2–3–4–5; suits never break ties.

In a party of N, first place earns N−1 points, second N−2, and so on. Tied hands split the average of the positions they occupy. Highest total after four rounds wins; tied totals share the result. Offline solo records each hand category from high card (1 point) to straight flush (9 points), without fabricated opponents or victory bonuses.

Optional assistance can be enabled in the lobby. Before you lock, you may privately turn one card face up per round. It costs one point that round even if you run out of time; other players see that assistance was used only at reveal. This is a memory game: no stakes, wagering, payments, or rewards.

## Episode-style duel

After two humans are connected, select **Episode-style duel** in Party setup. Both seats use the same 4×4 grid of sixteen cards, shown for 20 seconds from Enter match. After the grid is concealed, three further cards from the same deck appear and both players have 60 seconds to lock two different positions. Players can choose the same positions as each other. The stronger five-card poker hand wins. If the ranks and kickers tie exactly, both selections are shown briefly and a new deal begins. Neither seat can peek in this mode.

As an online-room adaptation, if only one seat locks by the deadline it wins by forfeit; if neither locks, the duel closes with no winner. Neither choice is revealed on a timeout. No ladder, nominations, Pieces, wagering, or elimination side rules are implemented here.

## What is verified

- Pure-engine tests cover deck uniqueness, hand ranks, kickers, repeat and malformed picks, tied and missed selections, phase boundaries, spectator projection, and peek privacy and cost.
- Two isolated browser contexts use the application's loopback protocol to complete four rounds via keyboard and touch, verify independent locks and exact totals, and rematch. This is not live PeerJS connectivity evidence.
- Episode-style duel tests verify timings, disjoint shared cards, private locks, malformed and stale commands, and tie redeals. The two-browser loopback test also checks that the first lock sends no unsolicited snapshot to the other seat.
- Previously seen study faces cannot be made secret again from a modified client. While playing, the browser host is authoritative and can inspect private state. Play with friends rather than relying on anti-cheat guarantees.
