# Art direction: The Strategy Chambers

The landing page and game illustrations use lantern-lit stone, warm ivory tabletop materials, sienna ink, restrained gold and crimson, and quiet engraving textures. The landing art introduces the venue rather than advertising a fictional playable game. Each chapter vignette has its own setting and recognizable objects.

## Artwork should teach the game

- Show the relevant objects at the scale that makes the rule legible: a knight's route, a counting rail, a notch in a tile, or the difference between revealed and concealed cards.
- Give related games a consistent material palette, but change the composition, characters, viewpoint and action. Do not make the same generic table in different colors.
- Use a short illustrated example or an interactive board when a picture explains more than prose. Keep rules text and accessible names for actions or state that artwork cannot convey.
- Keep action controls real HTML, readable over their backgrounds, operable by keyboard, and visible on narrow screens. Reduced motion is supported.
- The original illustrated chapter covers, later cover replacements, game visuals and rule demos are separate readiness checks. A title in the index is not necessarily playable.

## Publication checklist

- Art is drawn for Imp Plan. Source/reference images and third-party likenesses are not shipped.
- Validate opening composition and actual mechanics, 320 px/desktop crops, asset loading and reduced motion. Use real playable previews, never prerecorded footage presented as a playable board.
- Keep first-screen imagery efficient, prioritize the responsive hero, and defer chapter/game covers until needed. Avoid light text directly over an untested bright illustration.
